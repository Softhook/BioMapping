# Case and rev G board: what the outside review found

On 24 November 2026 an outside review of the printed back case (`cardputer_back.scad`), the rev G board and the firmware came in. I checked each point against the drawing, the board file, the STL files, the two photos and the code. This page lists the points that hold up and what to do about each one. They are grouped by when they matter: before printing, before ordering the board, on the real Cardputer, and small changes to the README. Points that turned out to be fine are near the end, with the reason. Letters such as "(review A)" point to the review's own sections.

**Status (24 November, later the same day):** items 1–19 are done in the drawing, the board and [README.md](README.md). For item 9 we chose (a): C9 is a tantalum part (Kyocera AVX TAJP106M010RNJ, LCSC C1952866). For item 8 the GPS moved 0.5 mm towards the headphone end and 0.5 mm towards the keyboard edge, which puts the H3 screw's head 10.2 mm from it. Items 7 (the cuff plug check) and 11–14 are now measurements and test steps in the README, for the real Cardputer. The four questions at the end are still open.

Positions are in the board's coordinates, as in [README.md](README.md): x runs along the Cardputer from the GPS end (0) to the headphone end (84), and y runs from the screen edge (0) to the keyboard edge (54), in mm.

## Before printing

### 1. The rim test prints as four loose pieces (review A)

**Checked: yes.** I counted the pieces in `cardputer_back_rim_test.stl`. There are four: the main wall, a loose corner between the EXT opening and the headphone slot, and the two screw posts as loose rings. The full back (`cardputer_back.stl`) is one piece, as it should be.

**Why:** the test is only the top 4 mm. The EXT opening (5.1 mm deep) and the headphone slot (5 mm) cut right through it, so the corner between them comes loose. The two posts stand on their own in the battery's space, so at that height nothing joins them to the wall. Loose rings can't show whether the screw holes line up, which is half of what Test 3 is for.

The review suggests a flat plate across the bottom of the test. That won't work at 4 mm: the battery fills that space down to 6 mm below the rim, so the plate would sit on the battery.

**Do:** make the test the whole battery layer (6.5 mm) plus a 1 mm plate exactly where M5Stack's floor was (6.5–7.5 mm below the rim). Nothing of the Cardputer can be in that place, because M5Stack's own floor was there. The walls, the loose corner and both posts all join the plate. As a bonus, it also tests that the battery fits. It takes about an hour to print instead of half an hour. Changes: `rim_test` in the drawing, and Test 3 in the README.

### 2. The roofs of the screw-head holes print in mid-air (review C)

**Checked: yes.** Each long screw's head sits in a 4.4 mm hole, and the screw carries on up through a 2.2 mm hole. Printed floor down, the roof of the 4.4 mm hole is a ring with nothing under it. That isn't a bridge, because a bridge has something to rest on at both ends. The ring will droop, and the screw head will then press on a rough, weak ledge. The README says "no supports are needed" and counts these as bridges, which is wrong for these two holes.

**Do:** close the 2.2 mm hole for one layer (0.2 mm) just above the head hole. The printer then bridges straight across the 4.4 mm hole, and the layers above stand on that bridge. After printing, push the screw (or a 2 mm drill) through the thin layer. This is one line in the drawing and one sentence in the README's printing notes.

### 3. The long screws' holes will print too small (not in the review)

On its way to the Cardputer, each long screw runs through 13 mm of 2.2 mm hole. Printed holes usually come out 0.1–0.3 mm small, so this one will be about 2.0 mm, the same size as the screw. M5Stack's 2.2 mm works because their back is moulded, and moulding is exact. In a print, the screw will cut its own thread into the case on the way through. When you then tighten it, the case's thread holds the screw in place, so it can't pull the case firmly onto the Cardputer.

**Do:** make the long screws' holes 2.4 mm in the drawing. This only removes plastic, so the check that the case is "nowhere thicker than M5Stack's" still holds. Or drill the printed holes out to 2.4 mm.

### 4. A small overhang in the EXT wall (review C)

**Checked: yes, but it's small.** In the bottom 1.5 mm of the battery layer, the wall on the EXT side is thinner (its inner face is at 1.2 mm instead of 1.75 mm), copying M5Stack's wall beside its Lego tubes. Where the wall gets thicker again, the inner face steps in by 0.55 mm. That prints as a small overhang along the whole wall. It will print, perhaps with a rough edge.

**Do:** replace the step with a 45° slope that starts at the step and rises from there. Don't fix it by making the thin band thicker: copying M5Stack's wall there is what guarantees nothing of the Cardputer touches it. A slope that rises from the step keeps that guarantee, because along the slope our wall is thinner than M5Stack's.

The review also flags the 1 mm strip of wall under the EXT opening. That is fine: it sits on the thicker wall beside the board.

### 5. The board's pocket is a little tight (review D)

**Checked: the numbers are right.** The gap between the board's edge and the case wall is 0.3 mm on each side. JLCPCB cuts board edges to within ±0.2 mm, and printed pockets usually come out a little small, so almost nothing is left. The posts have the same problem: they are 4 mm posts in 4.5 mm holes, 41.5 mm apart, and a print can shrink by a few tenths of a mm over that length.

**Do:**
- **Case:** widen the gap to 0.5 mm. Widen the shelf by the same 0.2 mm, so the board still rests on 0.7 mm of it. Only the wall beside the board gets thinner (1.6 → 1.4 mm). That part of the case is outside the Cardputer, so nothing inside the Cardputer is affected.
- **Board:** make H3 and H4 4.8 mm instead of 4.5 mm. This changes the board, so it must be done before ordering.

### 6. Board screws: use heat-set inserts (review D)

**Checked.** The drawing offers both options. The default is a screw that cuts its own thread in a 1.7 mm hole. Printed, that hole comes out nearer 1.5 mm. An M2 screw forced into it can split the post along its layers, and the thread wears out a little each time the case is opened. You will open it often during the tests.

**Do:** make `board_screw_inserts = true` the default, and buy M2 heat-set inserts (up to 4 mm long, for a 3.2 mm hole).

### 7. The cuff plug will be hard to grip (review B)

**Checked: yes.** The mouth of the cuff socket J2 is 0.75 mm inside the board's edge. That rule came from the older boards, which had no case around them. The outside of the case is another 1.9 mm further out (0.3 mm gap plus 1.6 mm wall). So the socket's mouth sits 2.7 mm deep inside a window about 10 × 6 mm. With the plug in, its back end may be roughly level with the outside of the case. With nothing to pinch, people will pull on the wires and tear them out of the plug.

Moving J2 outwards hardly helps. Its metal side tabs stick out 0.2 mm in front of the mouth, and they must stay clear of the board's edge. That is why it sits 0.75 mm in. At most 0.25 mm could be gained.

**Check first (two minutes):** push the cuff plug fully into a loose socket and measure how far its back end stands out from the socket's mouth. If it's less than about 5 mm, the plug needs this fix. While you're at it, measure the socket's height: the drawing guesses 6 mm, because KiCad has no 3D model of this part.

**Do:** cut a finger scoop into the outside of the case around the window: a shallow dip, about 16 mm wide and 2–3 mm deep, so a finger and thumb can reach the sides of the plug. Make the window 0.5 mm taller as well, so if its printed top sags a little it won't rub on the plug. Only the case changes.

## Before ordering the board

### 8. The GPS-to-screw check ignores the screw head (review I)

**Checked: yes.** `./pcb case` measures from the GPS module to the screw's shaft. For H3 that is 10.5 mm, just over u-blox's 10 mm. But the screw head is 2 mm wider than the shaft and sits at the same height as the module, so the nearest metal is really 9.5 mm away. The 10 mm is a guideline, not a cliff edge, so half a millimetre under it is very likely harmless.

**Do:** fix the check in `pcbtools/case.py` so it measures to the head for H3 and H4. Then, before ordering, either move the GPS about 0.6 mm further from H3 (if the board has room), or accept 9.5 mm. I'd move it if it's easy: it costs nothing now and can't be done later.

### 9. C9 can turn knocks into GSR jumps (review F)

**Checked: yes. The parts list and our own notes disagree.** `docs/cardputer_port.md` says to use a film or tantalum capacitor here, or to tap-test a ceramic one. The parts list has an ordinary 10 µF ceramic (X5R), the same part as the power capacitors.

**Why it matters for C9 and not the others:** C9 smooths the 0.5 V that is applied across the skin. Ordinary ceramic capacitors make tiny voltages when they are knocked. Anything added to that 0.5 V shows up directly in the GSR reading: a 1 mV blip is a 0.2 % jump in the reading. In the case, C9 sits right behind the keyboard and the speaker. The other 10 µF capacitors are on power lines, where a blip that small doesn't matter.

**Do (pick one):**
- **(a)** Fit a 10 µF tantalum capacitor in the same small size (0805, called "R case"), with its + end towards the 0.5 V. Tantalum doesn't react to knocks. It leaks a tiny current, which shifts the 0.5 V very slightly, but the bias check at the start of each recording measures that away. I'd choose this: it's what our notes already say. The footprint has no + mark, so add one to the board's printing.
- **(b)** Keep the ceramic, tap the first board during a recording with a fixed resistor, and keep a tantalum ready to swap in by hand.

The review also suggests a small 1–2 µF ceramic of the type that doesn't react to knocks (C0G). I wouldn't: it would let more noise from the power supply through, and that size is hard to buy.

The review also worried that C9 takes about a second to settle after switching on, so the first bias reading could be off. **Checked: not a problem.** Our firmware never switches the 5 V that feeds our board, so it comes on with the Cardputer. The ADC, and its first bias reading, only start when you start a session or the calibration, well after the start-up animation.

### 10. Two schematic notes overlap (review I)

**Checked: yes.** In `hardware/out/biomap_cardputer_sch.png`, the note at the bottom of block 4 runs into the note of block 5. Shorten block 4's note or split it over two lines, in `pcbtools/schematic.py`.

## On the real Cardputer: additions to the measurements and Test 1

### 11. Measurement 2: include the space in front of J1 (review E)

The first photo shows the Cardputer's flat-cable socket (labelled P1–P8) right in front of J1's mouth, at about x 65–69, y 9–16. There is also a white plastic clip of the front shell at the screen edge near J1, at about x 59–64. Our cable plug and the first bend of its wires stand about 4 mm above our board there, which leaves about 2.5 mm to the Cardputer's board. Measurement 2 already lists "the flat-cable socket beside it", but only asks what lies under J1 itself.

**Do:** add to measurement 2: the height of the flat-cable socket (with its cable) and of that white clip, and what lies under the plug and wires in front of J1 (x 55–70, y 2–16). Also measure how high the bent EXT legs stand: this matters for item 16.

### 12. Measurement 4: the battery's ribs had a job (review E)

Measurement 4 says that ribs inside M5Stack's back change "nothing: the board takes the floor's place". That isn't quite right. Our board does take the floor's place, but the ribs also stopped the battery sliding about, and our back has none. The battery could then slide a few mm, towards H2's screw head or the EXT legs.

**Do:** check whether the battery is stuck to the Cardputer (tape or glue). If it is, there's nothing to do. If not, stick two small foam pads on our board's inner face at the battery's ends, where M5Stack's ribs were.

### 13. New measurement: what holds the keyboard edge (review D)

Both long screws are 18 mm from the screen edge. Nothing holds the keyboard edge, 36 mm further on. That works for M5Stack's 8 mm back. Ours is almost three times as deep, so a pull on a strap, or gripping and twisting the device, can lever the keyboard edge open with much more force. M5Stack's 3D model shows no clips there, but models often leave such things out.

**Do:** look at M5Stack's real back. If it has clips, hooks or a lip along the keyboard edge, measure them, and I'll copy them into the drawing.

### 14. Test 1: add restarts (review H)

If the Cardputer restarts in the middle of reading the ADC, and the ADC stays powered, the ADC can get stuck holding its data wire low. After that, the Cardputer reports "ADC not found" until you switch it off and on again. This can happen after a crash, when new firmware is flashed over USB, or when M5Stack's start-up code briefly wiggles G3 and G5. Test 1 already switches the Cardputer off and on 10 times, but that switches the ADC off too, so it can't catch this.

**Do:** in Test 1, also restart the Cardputer 10 times while it is recording, without switching it off (for example by flashing it over USB), and check that the ADC is found every time. If it ever isn't, the standard fix is to send 9 pulses on the clock wire before opening the bus: about 15 lines in `gsr_cardputer.cpp`. I couldn't check whether M5Stack's library already does this, because its code isn't in the repository.

## Small changes to the README

**15. Cable colours (review G).** Ready-made GH cables often have a red wire on pin 1. On J1, pin 1 is a GPS wire (G15), and 5 V is pin 4. Wiring by colour habit would put 5 V into the GPS. Add to "The cable": ignore the wire colours. Before soldering, beep each wire from its pin on the plug (the pin numbers are printed beside J1) to the leg it is meant for.

**16. Kapton over the EXT legs (review E).** Step 3 of "Putting it together" says to put Kapton "around J1". Make it clear that the Kapton must cover the whole strip under the EXT legs (about x 32–52, y 5–18), as well as the area under the battery. The legs are bare metal, bent over the Cardputer's board, and one of them is 5VI, which is live whenever USB is plugged in. Our board's inner face is ground copper under a thin coat of solder mask, and a sharp point of solder could push through it.

**17. Closing the case (review E).** Add to "Putting it together", before the screws go in: the case must sit flat on the Cardputer by itself; check that no wire lies on the edge of the battery; never force the screws. A wire trapped under the battery would be pressed into it.

**18. Soldering G3 (review H).** Add a tip: slip a strip of Kapton between the two rows of legs while soldering G3, so solder can't reach 5VI.

**19. Screwdriver (review D).** The long screws' heads sit 9 mm deep in a 4.4 mm hole. Add a note to use a slim precision screwdriver, not a bit holder.

## Checked, and fine as it is

- **The thin upper part of the long posts (review C).** Tightening the screws squeezes these posts; it doesn't pull on them. If the case is levered, the pull goes through the screw and the thick lower part of the post, not the thin upper part. Making them thicker would also break the "nowhere thicker than M5Stack's" rule. Leave them as they are, and look again if item 13 shows the keyboard edge levering open.
- **The board is screwed down only near the screen edge (review D).** Once the case is closed, the battery stops the board lifting by more than 0.5 mm. Pushing the cuff plug in pushes along the board, not off it.
- **Power-up order (review H).** If the Cardputer drives its wire to the GPS (G13) high before our board's 3.3 V is up, a few mA leak into the GPS through R9. The prototype is wired the same way and its GPS starts fine. Only look at this if the GPS ever fails to start after switching on.
- **J1's latch is hard to reach (review E).** You unplug J1 with the case opened out beside the Cardputer, with the latch facing up. It's fiddly, but not a problem. A longer cable for probing the test pads on the bench is only worth making if it turns out to be needed.

## Questions for you

1. **Will anyone use a wrist strap?** The 1.8 mm printed strap pin (copied from M5Stack, whose pin is moulded) will snap under a real pull. If a strap will be used, I'd swap the pin for a short metal pin. It is far enough from the GPS. (review C)
2. **How many of these will be built?** Soldering G3 right behind 5VI is fine for one careful prototype, but slow and risky for helpers at a workshop. If it's more than a few, it's worth looking for another way to connect. (review H)
3. **A blank plug for the EXT socket?** Our wires now use five of its pins, so plugging an M5Stack add-on into it could clash with our board. The stock Cardputer has the same open socket, so this is optional: a printed blank plug, or a "don't use" label. (review I)
4. **How it is held.** The GPS faces out of the back, so it points into a palm, or at the ground when you look at the screen. This is the "screen-down" problem that's already on our list; it's worth deciding before the final case. (review I)
